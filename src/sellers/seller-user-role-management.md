# Seller Users & Roles

A seller doesn't have to run their store alone. Under **Settings**, a seller can invite **team members** and control what each one can do through **roles** — so staff get exactly the access they need in the Seller Panel, and nothing more.

::: info What you'll learn
- How roles define permissions
- How to add and manage team members
- The account-level settings a seller controls
:::

## Roles

Open **Settings → Roles**. A role is a named set of permissions. When you create one, you give it a **name** and **description** and choose a **Permission Type**:

<ImagePopup src="/images/seller-user-role-management/role-create.png" alt="Create seller role with permission tree" />

- **All** — full access to everything in the Seller Panel.
- **Custom** — pick exactly which areas the role can use from a **permission tree** covering Catalog, Inventories, Pricing, Marketing, Sales, Performance, Reporting, Communication, Customers, and Settings (each with granular sub-permissions like *Orders → Invoice* or *Products → Delete*).

<ImagePopup src="/images/seller-user-role-management/settings-roles.png" alt="Seller roles list" />

You can't switch the last remaining **All**-access role to Custom, or delete a role that's in use or your only role — so a seller can never lock themselves out.

## Users

Open **Settings → Users** and choose **Create User** to add a team member — their **name**, **email**, **phone**, **password**, the **role** that governs their access, and a **status** (active or suspended). Each user signs in to the Seller Panel with the permissions their role allows.

<ImagePopup src="/images/seller-user-role-management/settings-users.png" alt="Seller users list" />

::: info Staff limits
If your marketplace uses subscription plans, your plan's **Max Staff** caps how many users you can add — you're stopped once you reach it. See [Seller Subscriptions](/subscriptions/seller-subscription-management).
:::

## Account settings

**Settings → General** holds the seller's own account preferences — **preferred language**, **password** change, and whether to receive **communication emails**.

::: tip
Create roles first, then assign them to users — it's far easier than setting permissions per person, and keeps access consistent as your team grows.
:::
